# Design notes

Notes for picking this project up and carrying on. The README says *what* is here; these notes say *why* it is the way it is and *what to do next*.
Read these notes before you resume work.

Last updated: 2026-11-15

---

## 1. Goals and assumptions

- Use aluminium extrusion like a DIN rail, and keep adding parts, one per device, that hold Raspberry Pis, mini PCs, network routers, power strips and so on side by side.
- The extrusions are 20x20 and 40x40 (6 mm slot, 2 mm lip, 12 mm under the lips, 6 mm slot depth).
- There is also a version for real DIN rail (TS35 x 7.5, EN 60715: 35 mm wide, 7.5 mm deep, 1 mm sheet).
- **The back of the frame is assumed to be blocked.** Shapes that wrap around from the back cannot be used.
- The frame normally runs horizontally (as a rail). Mounting on a vertical frame has not been tested with the current one-piece design.
- Printed on an FDM printer without supports. Assumed conditions: 256 mm square build plate, 0.4 mm nozzle, PETG.

## 2. Decisions made by the maintainer (do not change)

| Decision | How it came about |
|---|---|
| Fixed to the frame without screws, and removable | Original requirement |
| Shapes are generated by a script, and the output is the same every time | Original requirement |
| The bracket is one piece. No two-part (A/B standard) design | See section 4, history 2 |
| L-shaped. The board sticks out forward from the frame, long side vertical | Picked option A from rough sketches A–D |
| The centre of the bracket is at the height of the frame centre | Maintainer's instruction |
| Can be mounted upside down (the grip is identical top and bottom) | Maintainer's instruction |
| The front plate does not stick out beyond the board outline | Maintainer's instruction |
| No gap between the frame front and the back plate. No spring structure | See section 4, history 3 |
| The front plate is notched where the SD card goes in and out | Maintainer's instruction |
| No text on the bottom face (the face on the build plate) | Maintainer's instruction |
| Standoffs are separate parts by default. Integrated standoffs are offered as an alternative | Maintainer's instruction |
| Board holes are 3 mm diameter (version without standoffs) | Maintainer's instruction |
| Width along the rail is 16 mm (the print height) | Reduced from 30 mm after the maintainer pointed it out |

## 3. Output format (keep it the same every time)

- **One model per file.** Output 3MF, STL and STEP.
- 3MF and STL files are in print orientation (front plate down), centred on a 256 mm square build plate.
- File names are `<board>_<2020|4040|DIN35>`, plus the same with `_spacer` appended. `<board>` is the board size: `PiB`, `PiA`, `PiZero`, `VIM1S` or `ROCK5B`.
- All generated models and `viewer.html` go in `models/` and are committed together with the scripts.
- `viewer.html` embeds three.js and opens without a network connection.
- `viewer.html` includes the full three.js MIT license (`vendor/LICENSE-three.txt`) as an HTML comment, so the notice is not lost when `viewer.html` is copied on its own.

## 4. History (rejected ideas and why)

Kept so the same ideas are not proposed again.

### History 1: choosing from four mounting methods → dropped

At first the mounting method was chosen by conditions from four options: snap-fit, slide-in from the end, clamp-around and twist-lock.
When the frame is used like a DIN rail, the value is that every device goes on and comes off the same way. A different method per device throws that away, so a single mounting method was chosen.

### History 2: two-part design (A/B standard) → dropped

A standard was designed with a frame-side part A (fixed shape) and a device-side part B (designed per device), with B sliding down into A.

- In the dovetail version, the upper edge of the groove in part A started in mid-air, so it could not be printed without supports.
- The printability check at the time only looked at face angles, missed this and wrongly reported "no supports needed" (fixed in the check method in section 6).
- Switching to a 45-degree French cleat made it printable, but nothing stopped it from coming out: it lifted off when pulled forward.
- On the maintainer's suggestion it became one piece. The print orientation of the L (front plate down) matches that of the grip (rail direction up), so a single part prints without trouble.

**Limits of the one-piece design**: the device cannot be removed on its own. Each frame size needs a separate part. Devices whose print orientation does not match the grip (flat parts you would want to print lying down) may not work as one piece.

### History 3: how the grip evolved

| Version | How it grips | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Top: rigid hook, bottom: spring clip | The top takes the weight | Made identical top and bottom after the maintainer pointed it out |
| Identical flexible hooks top and bottom (8 mm to the back plate) | Hooks set 8 mm forward to gain flex length | Tightened up after the maintainer pointed it out |
| Folded spring (3 mm to the back plate) | Hooks folded back along the back of the back plate | **Too loose in a test print; did not stay on** |
| No gap, stiff arms (current) | The back of the back plate sits directly on the frame front | Weak engagement in a test print → barb raised to 1.0 mm |

- The key that sits in the front slot was added on the maintainer's suggestion. The key takes the vertical load, and the hooks only keep the bracket from coming off.
- Raising the barb to 1.0 mm increases how far the arms open, so the arms were tapered from 1.6 mm at the root to 0.9 mm at the tip. A rough beam calculation gives about 3.1% strain and about 61 N push-on force per arm.

### History 4: adding the DIN rail version

The same front plate as the frame version, with a grip for DIN rail.

- **The frame grip cannot be reused**: the rail lips are thin and shallow (1 mm), so arms coming straight off the back plate cannot get enough flex length (the strain is far too high).
- **Leaf springs**: the hooks sit at the tips of cantilevered leaf springs that run along the rail (the print height direction). The leaf springs are separated from the back plate by a 0.5 mm gap and joined only by the 3 mm on the build plate side. They flex vertically (x), so they print in the same orientation.
- **Centre key**: sits in the rail channel (between the side walls) and takes the vertical load rigidly. Same idea as the key in the front slot of the frame version. The leaf springs only keep the bracket from coming off.
- **How this differs from the "spring structure with a gap" avoided earlier**: what was avoided was a gap between the back plate and the frame, which caused play. The back plate of the DIN version sits directly on the rail front; the only gaps are beside the leaf springs.
- A rough beam calculation gives about 1.7% strain in the leaf springs and about 4.8 N push-on force each.

### History 5: shorter print time (version 1 → 2)

| Change | Effect on time (estimate) |
|---|---|
| Remove the bosses (standoffs) | The largest. Many layers of small circles |
| Make the back plate a low rim on the outer parts | About −1% |
| Fewer cross ribs | About −3% (not adopted, it reduces stiffness) |

- Most of the time goes into printing walls; removing volume does not save much time.
- The number of layers is set by the width along the rail (16 mm). Making it narrower reduces the engagement of the hooks and the key, so it was left alone.

### History 6: the whole Raspberry Pi range (2026-11-15)

The maintainer asked for brackets for every Raspberry Pi, and for STL files as well.

- **One bracket per board size**, not one per model. Boards with the same outline and mounting holes on their official drawings would get identical files under different names, so they share one: the former `Pi4` became `PiB` (Raspberry Pi 5 / 4 B / 3 B+ / 3 B / 2 B / 1 B+), and `PiA` was added for the 3 A+ / 1 A+. `PiZero` covers the Zero, Zero W and Zero 2 W. The geometry of `PiB` is unchanged from `Pi4` (same volumes).
- `PiA` follows `PiZero` (one hole set that fits either way round, SD notches at both ends, two windows) with the window band of `PiB` (clear of the 6 mm standoffs).
- **Not covered**: Raspberry Pi 1 Model A / B (no official mechanical drawing, so the holes cannot be checked), Compute Modules (they need a carrier board), Raspberry Pi 400 / 500 (keyboard computers), Raspberry Pi Pico (a microcontroller board).
- STL files are written next to 3MF and STEP, with the same placement as the 3MF and the same tessellation (0.02 mm, 0.2 rad). About 270 KB each.

### History 7: popular boards from other manufacturers (2026-11-15)

The maintainer asked for popular boards from other manufacturers, in the same vertical orientation as the Raspberry Pi brackets, with STL files too.

- **Existing brackets first.** A board whose outline and mounting holes match an existing bracket on its official drawing or 3D model gets no new files, only its name in the class title and the README: Radxa ROCK 3C and Pine64 ROCK64 → `PiB`, Radxa ZERO 3W / 3E → `PiZero`, Khadas VIM3 / VIM4 → `VIM1S`.
- **The VIM3 uses the `VIM1S` bracket.** A first survey said one VIM3 hole differs (53.5 mm where the VIM1S has 45.5 mm). On the official DXF and 3D model the holes are identical to the VIM1S; the 53.5 mm is measured from the board edge to the far hole, not between holes.
- **ZERO 3W / 3E**: the holes on the official DXF are up to 0.14 mm away from the Raspberry Pi Zero positions (3.55 / 3.6 / 61.4 mm along the board and 3.6 / 26.45 / 26.5 mm across, against 3.5 / 61.5 and 3.5 / 26.5). The board holes are 2.8 mm, so an M2.5 screw still has 0.15 mm of play on each side and reaches the `PiZero` standoffs. The DXF puts the holes on a layer named `PIN_TOP`; the official 3D models confirm they are the mounting holes (through the PCB at the same positions).
- **`--board-step` replaces `--vim1s-step`.** Each board class lists the official 3D models it fits in `STEPS` (rotation, centre of the PCB outline and height of its underside, in the file's own coordinates), and `sbc_brackets.official()` places any of them on its bracket. The placement was checked by comparing the holes in each model with the bracket holes: VIM3 / VIM4 exact, ZERO 3W / 3E within 0.14 mm, ROCK 5B / 5B+ within 0.06 mm. The VIM1S result is unchanged from before.
- **Models that are partly surfaces.** The ROCK 5B+ model (its PCB among others) and parts of the VIM1S / VIM4 / ZERO models are shells that belong to no solid. `--check` counts their area that lies strictly inside the bracket (faces lying on the bracket's surface, such as the board resting on the standoffs, are not counted).
- **ZERO 3E**: the pads under the holes stand 0.02 mm proud of the PCB underside in the official model, so its underside height in `STEPS` is the pad level (otherwise the pads overlap the standoff tops by 1.30 mm3).
- **ROCK 5B / 5B+ get their own bracket (`ROCK5B`).** 100.2 x 74.25 mm, four 3.5 mm holes 93.15 x 49 mm apart, 3.55 mm from the GPIO-side edge (the survey had guessed 58 x 49 mm).
  - Standoffs are 8 mm: the deepest parts under the ROCK 5B+ reach 5.4 mm below the board, and the ROCK 5B's microSD card sits under it. An SSD fitted in the M.2 slots under the board is not in the official models and has not been checked.
  - Standoffs are 5.4 mm in diameter, M2.5 with a 2.2 mm pilot hole. A part under the 5B+ is 3.1 mm from a hole centre; a 7 mm standoff overlapped it.
  - Board holes in the version without standoffs are 3 mm, as on the other brackets.
  - No SD notch: the microSD slot is at the GPIO-side edge on the underside. The ROCK 5B's card (in its official model) sticks out 9.8 mm past that edge, above the front plate and beside the frame; on the 40x40 version it clears the slope of the back plate by 0.42 mm. The ROCK 5B+ model has no card in the slot; its slot is in the same place, so the card should sit the same way, but this has not been checked against a model.
  - Two rows of lightening windows, clear of the standoffs.
- **Not added**: Libre Computer AML-S905X-CC (Le Potato). Its product page gives 85 x 56 mm and holes 58 x 49 mm but not their distance from the edges; it has not been checked against the official 3D model yet. The ROCK 3C and ROCK64 have no official 3D model, so only their outline, holes and microSD position were compared on the drawings.

## 6. Checks and caveats

- **Printability (supports needed or not)**: use `checks_print.py`. It slices the part layer by layer and checks that each layer stays within 45 degrees of the outline of the layer below. **Do not rely on face angles alone**: that misses shapes that start in mid-air (history 2).
- **Interference**: measure the volume of the intersection between the bracket and the frame, and between the bracket and the board. Use the board's official 3D model when there is one.
- **Retention**: move the bracket 0.3 mm forward, up and down, and confirm it then overlaps the frame (i.e. it is hooked on).
- **Upside down**: confirm zero interference in the flipped position too.
- **Print time**: `checks_time.py` is a home-made estimate, not a slicer measurement. Use it only to compare shapes. No slicer measurements have been taken yet.
- **Regression check**: after changing the script, confirm the models you did not mean to change still have the same volume.

### Checking the DIN rail dimensions (2026-11-14)

The centre key (`DIN_KEY_HALF`) sits in the hat's channel (between the inner faces of the side walls). The inner width of the channel is "outer width of the base − side wall thickness × 2".

| Source | Rail | Outer width of the base | Side wall thickness | Inner width of the channel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [AutomationDirect DINnector DN-R35 drawing](https://cdn.automationdirect.com/static/specs/dinrails.pdf) | Steel, 7.5 deep | 27.0 | 1.0 | 25 (calculated) |
| [Takachi Electronics Enclosure FDR series drawing](https://www.takachi-el.co.jp/assets/attachments/images/din%20rail%202022-2024.pdf) (p. 14-33) | Aluminium, 7.3 deep | 27 | 1.5 | 24 (on the drawing) |

- The tolerance of the base width could not be confirmed from available documents. Annex A.3 of IEC 60715:2017 (Figures A.3 and A.4), which defines the rail profiles, is not in the [public preview](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/23197/de6fcc085faa4dc1a9af3ef4265d161d/IEC-60715-2017.pdf). Figure 2 (TH 35), which is in the preview, gives device-side dimensions and only shows the lip thickness 1 ±0.04 and the width 35 ±0.3.
- The drawing in Weidmüller's TS 35X7.5 datasheet only gives width 35, depth 7.5 and thickness 1, not the base width.
- On a steel rail with 1 mm sheet, the clearance between the key (24.4 mm wide) and each side wall is 0.3 mm.
- **The current key does not fit aluminium rails with 1.5 mm side walls (7.3 mm deep, 24 mm channel).** Overlaid on a rail with 1.5 mm side walls, all three DIN models interfere by 10.24 mm3 (the key hits by 0.2 mm on each side). The lip edge where the hooks catch is 1 mm thick, the same as TS35 x 7.5.

- Take dimensions for new boards from the manufacturer's official 3D model or drawing. Do not place holes from memory or guesswork: for the VIM1S, the first guess ("rectangular pattern") was wrong.
- Official 3D models and drawings are not included (`*.stp`, and `*.step` at the top level, are excluded in `.gitignore`). To re-check interference, download them from the manufacturers' sites (links in the table above) and pass each with `--board-step`. The file name tells which bracket it belongs to (`STEPS` in each board class).
- Shapes converted from the official 3D models do not go into `viewer.html` either. `--board-step` is used only for interference checks, and the boards in the viewer are bare plates, because no notice allowing redistribution of the manufacturers' CAD data could be found (Khadas as of 2026-11-14; Radxa and Pine64 as of 2026-11-15).
